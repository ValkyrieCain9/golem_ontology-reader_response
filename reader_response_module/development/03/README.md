# Iteration 3 - Motivating Scenario

## Name
Discourse Acts and Discourse Structure

## Description
Any sort of response can be characterised in its broadest sense as an act of communication and the specific exchange of ideas between two or more actors can be seen more specifically as an act of discourse. Zhang et al. (2017) provide a set of 9 tags for characterising the different types of discourse acts - announcement (which we will refer to as a statement), question/request, answer, agreement, disagreement, appreciation, negative reaction, elaboration and humour.

This initial tag set, which was created from an analysis of a wide variety of Reddit communities, can be further extended and adapted to apply more specifically to the discourse of creative works across different platforms.

First is the addition of the evaluation tag which can be thought of as the act of communicating quality. This is particularly prevalent on review websites like Goodreads. Rebora and Vezzani (2024) provide a specific structure for characterising evaluation types on Goodreads, grouping them into three main categories - individual evaluation, aesthetic evaluation and social evaluation. While these three categories were made with evaluation in mind, they can be applied to overall discourse of creative and literary works.

Another addition to the discourse set would be a tag for suggestion/recommendation acts. This is particularly useful on websites like AO3 where readers can sometimes provide change/correction suggestions to the writers.

With the different types of discourse acts established, further insight can be drawn from how different acts relate to each other in a given act of communication. Such discourse act relations will be influenced by the website’s social function (reviewing, writing and sharing, general discussion etc) as well as the natural discourse structure of the website.

The most common discourse structure is the thread, in which comments and their replies form nested trees. Lui et al. (2026) define four types of threads - post thread (the total collection of comments under a post), top-level comment thread which has as its parent the post itself, nested thread which has as its parent a top-level comment, and deep-nested thread which has as its parent a nested thread comment.

As Goodreads is primary a platform for leaving reviews about books, the reviews can be generally categorised as evaluation acts. Here each evaluation act can be further broken down into smaller clauses, each their own evaluation act (or possibly also statement and elaboration pairs) with a specific creative discourse theme as described by Rebora and Vezzani (2024). Each of these smaller acts can also be assigned a degree of positive or negative, with the overall review being a positive evaluation.

“However, some of the military conflict sections significantly slowed it down for me.” - Individual experience, negative

“The scenes in which they converse are by far the most compelling” - textual and metatextual material, positive

“If you are interested in Necrons, this is possibly the best work of fiction you could ask for that explores their nature.” - social impact, positive

AO3 has examples of top-level comment threads and even nested threads, allowing for more interaction between the users, particularly between the author of the fan fiction and the readers. Here, the first user, a reader, performs an act of appreciation about the textual and meta textual material of the work. They also include an act of suggestion, pointing out a typo to the author.

The second user, the author, responds to this discourse act with both an act of appreciation and an answer (response) to the readers suggestion.

Reddit has examples of deep-nested threads and therefore works well to show more complex chains of discourse relations. Here we consider the discourse acts just at the comment level (not the clause level).

The top-level comment is responding to the initial post discussing a particular anime. The thread can therefore be described in terms of discourse acts as follows

—> Statement

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—> suggestion

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—> statement (rhetorical question) - textual and meta textual, positive

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—> agreement - textual and meta textual, positive

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—> elaboration - textual and meta textual, positive

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;—> humour

While the initial top-level comment was not directly related to the discussion post or to the anime in question, the second nested comment and the deep nested comments that follow speak more directly to the anime and can therefore also be assigned the creative discourse themes.
