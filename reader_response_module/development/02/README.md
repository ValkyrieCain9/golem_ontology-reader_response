## Name
Digital Objects

## Description
As comments, and the posts they respond to, are created, shared and stored within the digital space, they need to be understood as digital objects. Faulkner and Runde (2019) provide a set of definitions to describe the nature of the digital object, taking into account both its material and immaterial aspects. A digital object is described as a bitstring, a type of immaterial syntactic expression, which is borne by some sort of material or immaterial bearer. Bitstrings are subdivided into program files (software and computing) and data files (data and documents). 

A digital object can be formed from a layering of several different bearers, but at least the bottom most layer must be a material carrier in order for the digital object to be used, stored or communicated. Translation actions are the set of practices which describe the representation of a digital object from one structural layer to another. 

The properties of a particular digital object are set by what Faulkner and Runde describe as an object's social position. The social position is what describes an object's expected functions, rights and responsibilities attributed to that object. This also defines what sort of translation actions can be performed on the digital object.

*Faulkner, P., & Runde, J. (2019). Theorizing the Digital Object. MIS Quarterly, 43(4), 1279–1302.*

## Example 1
We can think of the internet as one of the largest material (in terms of servers) and immaterial (in terms of networking software) bearers of digital objects, the most common of these digital objects being a website through which most people interact with the digital structure of the internet. 

The website is made of HTML encoding, interacting with layers of web technology program files (e.g. CSS and JavaScript). Here a website’s social position influences the structure, formatting and function and therefore influences the type of digital objects which it bears. 

**YouTube** – social website for sharing videos – videos, audio, text </br>
**AO3** – community-run website for sharing fanfiction – text </br>
**Reddit** – website for forum communities of any kind – video, images, text </br>
**GitHub** – website for cloud storage of software – csv files, Json files, html files, python scripts etc. </br>


## Example 2
A website is formally made up of a collection of webpages, which provide a way for the immaterial information of a website to be organised into specific categories. Webpages provide another layer of social positioning, further refining the type of digital objects they have, the subject of their information as well as the ways in which users can interact with them.

Reddit has separate webpages for each of its communities, known as subreddits. Each subreddit is centred around a particular theme, dictating the type of objects that can be found, as well as a set of rules (normally defined by volunteer moderators) defining user behaviour in terms of contributing to the page and interacting with other users.

## Example 3

On websites like Reddit, AO3 and YouTube, users can often contribute to a webpage in one of two ways, posting or commenting. The two actions overlap in that they add an instance of a digital object to a webpage by a user. The difference comes in that posts often exist as standalone objects (following the social position of the webpage) while comments are objects responding to posts and therefore always exist together with a post object. Furthermore, posts can often be made of any type of data file object (video, text, image etc) while comments often exist in text form, depending on the structure and of the website and webpage.
